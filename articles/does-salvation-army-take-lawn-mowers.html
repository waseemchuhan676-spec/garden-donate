Does Salvation Army Take Lawn Mowers? Acceptance Rules & Donation Guide

  • ✓
    Working Mowers Accepted: Clean, safe mowers in running order are widely accepted.
  • ✓
    Free Truck Pickup: Available in select ZIP codes for large items like riding mowers.
  • ✕

When a lawn mower is too good to throw away but no longer fits your yard routine, The Salvation Army is often one of the first organizations donors think of. Its family stores sell donated goods to fund adult rehabilitation and community programs, and its truck pickup service can collect large items from your home in many areas. That combination makes it a strong option for mower donations, but it is not a guaranteed yes at every location.

Unlike a national chain with one intake rule, Salvation Army donation centers operate regionally. What one city accepts, another may decline, and pickup availability depends on your ZIP code. This guide explains which mowers Salvation Army locations usually accept, how pickup works, how to prepare your mower, and what to do if your local center says no. If you are also comparing other charities, see our guides on whether Goodwill takes lawn mowers and where to donate garden tools.

Branch Verification Notice: Acceptance and pickup rules vary by local Salvation Army center. Always confirm with your local center before loading a mower or scheduling a pickup. Never assume a center accepts gas-powered equipment without checking its local policy first.

Direct Answer: Does Salvation Army Take Lawn Mowers?

Direct Answer

Yes, at Many Locations — With Conditions

The Salvation Army accepts lawn mowers at many donation centers, but acceptance depends on condition, power type, and local branch policy:

  • Working push mowers: Widely accepted when clean, safe, and in running order. Push-reel, cordless electric, and drained gas walk-behind mowers are the easiest to place.
  • Riding mowers: Accepted at select centers only, and usually through scheduled truck pickup rather than store drop-off, because of size and transport needs.
  • Non-working mowers: Usually rejected by retail donation centers, which do not repair equipment. Vocational programs are a better route for these (see below).
  • Fuel rule: Gas mowers must have fuel and oil drained before acceptance at most centers, for fire safety and warehouse storage rules.

What Kind of Mowers Does Salvation Army Accept?

Salvation Army family stores resell donated items, so they judge a mower the way a buyer would: does it start, is it safe, and can it be sold as-is? The table below reflects the most common pattern across centers, but your local center has the final word.

Mower type Usually accepted? What to know
Push-reel (manual) mower Yes No fuel or battery hazards; blades must be intact and safe.
Cordless electric mower Yes Include the battery and charger, or the mower is far harder to resell.
Gas walk-behind mower Select centers Fuel and oil must be fully drained; mowers smelling of gasoline are commonly turned away.
Riding mower / lawn tractor Select centers Usually pickup-only due to size; must be working and transportable.
Non-working mower (any type) Usually no Retail centers do not repair equipment; try vocational schools or scrap recycling.

Salvation Army Pickup for Lawn Mowers

Pickup is one of Salvation Army's biggest advantages for mower donors, especially for riding mowers that do not fit in a car. Here is how it normally works:

  • Schedule online: Visit satruck.org, the official Salvation Army pickup scheduler, enter your ZIP code, and list your items. Pickup is offered in select ZIP codes only, so availability depends on your area.
  • What qualifies: Large items, including riding mowers and multi-item donations, are the most likely to qualify for a truck visit. A single push mower may be drop-off only in some areas.
  • Prepare for the truck: Place the mower where the crew can reach it safely, drain fuel and oil beforehand if it is a gas model, and keep keys, batteries, and chargers with the machine.
  • Receipt: Ask the crew for a donation receipt at pickup. Keep it for your records; tax deductibility depends on the organization's qualification and your own tax situation, so consult a qualified tax professional.
  • Timing: Pickup windows vary by region and demand. If you need the mower gone by a specific date, schedule early and have a backup plan (such as a drop-off center) in case your slot is delayed.

If online scheduling shows no availability in your ZIP code, call your local Salvation Army center directly. Some centers arrange pickups by phone even when the online tool shows none, and they can confirm exactly what their truck can take.

How to Prepare Your Mower for Salvation Army Drop-Off

A little preparation dramatically increases the chance your mower is accepted on the first visit:

  • Clean it: Remove caked grass, soil, and debris from the deck, wheels, and handles. A clean mower signals that it was maintained.
  • Drain gas and oil: For gas models, drain the fuel tank and engine oil following the manufacturer's manual and local disposal rules. Never pour fuel or oil down a drain or onto soil.
  • Test it: Start the mower before you leave home. If it will not start, be honest about it, and consider the vocational-school route below instead of a retail center.
  • Bundle accessories: Include the battery and charger for electric models, the grass bag or mulch plug if you have them, and the safety key for riding mowers.
  • Check safety parts: Blade brakes, kill switches, and guards should be intact. Missing safety parts are a common reason for rejection.
  • Bring help for riders: Riding mowers are heavy. If you are dropping one off, bring ramps and another person, and confirm the center has space before you travel.

What If Salvation Army Cannot Take Your Mower?

A rejection is not the end of the road. Depending on the mower's condition, these alternatives often work:

  • Habitat for Humanity ReStore: Many ReStores accept working mowers, and some offer pickup for large items. Verify with your local store first.
  • Goodwill: Many locations accept working push and electric mowers; gas mowers must be drained. See our Goodwill lawn mower guide for the detailed rules.
  • Vocational and agricultural programs: High school shops, community college small-engine classes, and repair clinics often welcome non-working mowers as training projects.
  • Community gardens and grounds programs: A working mower can serve a community garden or neighborhood grounds crew directly. Ask about current need before delivering.
  • Give-away platforms: Freecycle and Buy Nothing groups let a neighbor collect a working mower directly, which solves the transport problem for large riders.
  • Scrap recycling: For mowers that are unsafe or beyond repair, scrap metal recycling is the responsible last resort. Drain all fluids first and follow local hazardous-waste rules for batteries and fuel.

Tax Receipts: What to Know

The Salvation Army is a qualified nonprofit organization, and donations to it may be tax-deductible if you itemize deductions, subject to IRS rules and limits. The organization cannot tell you what your mower is worth; fair market value is your responsibility to document. Frequently Asked Questions

Does Salvation Army take lawn mowers?

Yes, many Salvation Army donation centers accept lawn mowers, but acceptance varies by location. Working push mowers in clean, safe condition are the most widely accepted. Gas mowers usually must have fuel and oil fully drained, and riding mowers are accepted only at select centers, often through truck pickup. Always verify with your local center before visiting.

Does Salvation Army pick up lawn mowers from homes?

Yes, in select ZIP codes. Pickup is scheduled through satruck.org, the official Salvation Army pickup service. Large items such as riding mowers are the most likely to qualify for a truck visit, while a single push mower may be drop-off only in some areas. If the online tool shows no availability, call your local center to ask about phone scheduling.

Does Salvation Army take riding lawn mowers?

Some centers do, usually via scheduled pickup rather than store drop-off, because of size and weight. The rider should be working, transportable, and have its fuel drained if it is a gas model. Confirm space and pickup availability with your local center before arranging anything.

Do I need to drain gas before donating a mower to Salvation Army?

In most cases, yes. Gas-powered mowers generally must have gasoline and engine oil fully drained before acceptance, for fire safety and warehouse storage rules. Follow the manufacturer's manual and local disposal regulations when draining fluids, and never pour fuel or oil onto soil or into drains.

Will Salvation Army take a lawn mower that does not start?

Usually not at retail donation centers, which do not repair equipment. Non-working mowers are better directed to vocational schools, community college small-engine programs, or community repair clinics that use them as training projects. If the mower is unsafe or beyond repair, scrap metal recycling is the responsible option.

Does Salvation Army take other garden tools?

Many centers accept usable hand tools, such as shovels, rakes, and pruners, along with other household goods. Acceptance still varies by location and condition. For a full comparison of donation destinations, see our guide on where to donate garden tools.

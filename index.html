When donation may not be appropriate

  • Exposed electrical wiring or serious safety defects
  • Fuel, oil or hazardous-fluid leaks
  • Severe structural damage
  • Known safety recalls that have not been addressed
  • Hazardous chemicals or containers

Garden Tool Donation Guide

Practical steps for giving garden tools, lawn equipment and gardening supplies a useful second life.

1. Where to start

For usable garden tools, start with local reuse programs, donation organizations, community gardens, tool libraries and nonprofits. The best option depends on your location and the item you have.

2. Where to donate old or used garden tools

Used tools in safe, usable condition may be suitable for local reuse organizations, community gardens, tool libraries or charities. Contact the specific location before making a trip.

3. Search by ZIP code or city

Use the finder above to create location-specific search options. This page does not claim that a particular local branch accepts your item unless that branch confirms it.

4. What to do with broken or rusty garden tools

Light surface rust or normal wear may not prevent reuse, but severely damaged or unsafe tools may need repair, recycling or another appropriate disposal route. Ask the receiving organization first.

5. Can I donate a lawn mower?

Some organizations accept lawn mowers, but powered equipment can have stricter requirements. Confirm acceptance, condition, fuel requirements and transport instructions before donating.

6. Can I donate gardening supplies?

Clean reusable pots, planters, hoses, stakes and similar supplies may be useful to community gardens or other reuse programs. Confirm current needs before dropping them off.

7. Keep donation documentation

If you need a receipt or acknowledgment, request it from the receiving organization. This website does not determine tax deductibility or official donation values.

Frequently Asked Questions

Where can I donate garden tools?

Possible options include reuse centers, Habitat ReStore locations, Goodwill organizations, community gardens, schools, tool libraries and nonprofits. Acceptance varies by location.

Where can I donate garden tools near me?

Use a ZIP code or city search to find local donation and reuse options. Contact the exact location before visiting.

Where can I donate old or used garden tools?

Try local reuse centers, community gardens, tool libraries, nonprofits, Goodwill organizations or Habitat ReStore locations. Confirm the local policy first.

Does Goodwill accept garden tools?

Goodwill organizations set their own local donation policies. Some locations may accept tools and equipment, while others may not.

Does Habitat ReStore accept garden tools?

ReStore donation guidelines vary by location. Some may accept usable tools or home-improvement items. Check the official local policy first.

Can I donate a lawn mower?

Some organizations accept lawn mowers, but requirements vary. Confirm condition, fuel requirements and pickup or drop-off rules.

Can I donate gardening supplies?

Some community gardens, nonprofits and reuse programs may accept clean reusable gardening supplies. Confirm current needs first.

What can I do with broken or rusty garden tools?

Light wear may be acceptable, but severely broken or unsafe tools may need repair, recycling or another appropriate disposal route.

Should I clean garden tools before donating?

Yes. Remove soil, grass and loose debris, dry the item, inspect it for damage and keep accessories together when possible.

Can donated garden tools qualify for a tax deduction?

A donation may qualify under applicable tax rules when requirements are met. Keep appropriate documentation and consult current official tax guidance or a qualified tax professional.

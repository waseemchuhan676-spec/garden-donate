Garden Tool Donation Guide

Practical steps for giving garden tools and lawn equipment a useful second life.

1. Identify the item

Choose the closest category: hand tools, lawn equipment, power tools, gardening supplies or large equipment. Different organizations may have different acceptance requirements.

2. Check condition

Clean, usable and safe items are generally easier for organizations to process. Power equipment deserves extra attention because electrical, mechanical and fuel-related hazards can affect acceptance.

3. Search by ZIP or city

Use the finder above to organize your search. This site currently provides a discovery workflow and official-source starting points; it does not claim that a particular local branch accepts your item unless that branch confirms it.

4. Consider community reuse

Community gardens, schools, youth programs, tool libraries and local reuse programs can sometimes use gardening supplies directly. Contact them before transporting items.

5. Prepare lawn equipment carefully

Lawn mowers, blowers, trimmers and other equipment can have stricter rules. Confirm acceptance and follow the recipient's transport and safety instructions.

6. Keep donation documentation

If you need a receipt or acknowledgment, request it from the receiving organization. This website does not determine tax deductibility or official donation values.

Frequently Asked Questions

Where can I donate garden tools?

Possible options include reuse centers, Habitat ReStores, Goodwill organizations, community gardens, schools, tool libraries and nonprofits. Acceptance varies by location.

Can I donate a lawn mower?

Some organizations accept lawn mowers, but requirements vary. Confirm that the exact location accepts your mower and whether it must be working.

Can I donate broken garden tools?

Severely broken or unsafe tools may be better suited to repair, reuse or recycling. Ask the receiving organization before dropping them off.

Can I donate rusty garden tools?

Light surface rust may be repairable, but severe rust or structural damage can make a tool unsuitable. The receiving organization decides what it accepts.

Can I get a tax deduction for donated tools?

Potentially, if applicable requirements are met. This website does not provide tax advice or determine deductibility.

Should I clean garden tools before donating?

Yes. Remove soil, grass and loose debris, dry the item and keep accessories together when possible.

Where to Donate a Riding Lawn Mower: Pickup, Rules & Alternatives

A push mower fits in a car trunk. A riding mower does not. That single difference changes everything about donating one: which organizations can take it, whether anyone will collect it, and how much preparation matters. If you have a lawn tractor or riding mower you no longer need, this guide walks through the donation options that actually work for large machines, including pickup services, and what to do when the rider no longer runs.

Riding mowers are also among the most valuable items a donor can give. A working rider can serve a community grounds crew, a vocational program, or a family that cannot afford one for years. The challenge is matching a heavy, bulky machine with an organization that has the truck, space, and need for it. For push mowers and handheld equipment, see our guides on whether Goodwill takes lawn mowers and where to donate garden tools.

Branch Verification Notice: Riding mower acceptance and pickup depend heavily on the local organization. Space, truck availability, and intake rules vary by location. Confirm with the specific organization before scheduling anything, and never assume a drop-off store can receive a rider.

Direct Answer: Where Can You Donate a Riding Lawn Mower?

Direct Answer

Yes — Start With Pickup-Capable Organizations

A working riding mower can be donated, but your best options are organizations with trucks, not ordinary drop-off stores:

  • Habitat for Humanity ReStore: Often the best fit — many ReStores accept working riders and some offer pickup for large items or multi-item donations.
  • The Salvation Army: Strong option where available — truck pickup in select ZIP codes via satruck.org; see our Salvation Army lawn mower guide.
  • Goodwill: Varies widely — many stores are drop-off only and cannot receive riders; verify before traveling.
  • Vocational programs: Best for non-running riders — schools and repair clinics use them as training projects.
  • Non-running or unsafe riders: Scrap recycling is the responsible last resort after fluids are drained.

Best Places to Donate a Riding Lawn Mower

BUILDING & HARDWARE REUSE

1. Habitat for Humanity ReStore

ReStores sell donated building materials, appliances, and outdoor equipment to fund affordable housing. Because they already handle bulky goods, they are among the most realistic recipients for a working rider.

Intake criteria: The rider should be working, structurally sound, and safe to operate. Fuel and oil rules vary by store; many require gas models to be drained. Pickup may be available for large items at some locations, but push-style drop-off rules do not apply here — call first and describe the machine, including its size and whether it runs.

CHARITABLE TRUCK PICKUP

2. The Salvation Army

Salvation Army family stores are supported by a truck pickup network that makes them a practical choice for riders, which most donors cannot transport themselves.

Intake criteria: Pickup is offered in select ZIP codes through satruck.org. Working riders in reasonable condition are the most likely to be accepted. Gas models generally need fuel and oil drained. If online scheduling shows no availability in your area, call your local center — phone scheduling sometimes differs from the online tool.

CHARITABLE THRIFT STORE

3. Goodwill

Goodwill's position on riding mowers is the least consistent of the major charities, because stores are run regionally and many are drop-off only with limited outdoor space.

Intake criteria: Some larger Goodwill locations accept working riders; many smaller stores cannot. Gas riders must have fuel and oil fully drained. Because the answer is genuinely local, verify with your specific store before arranging transport, and read our Goodwill lawn mower rules for the full picture.

EDUCATION & TRAINING

4. Vocational Schools and Agricultural Programs

High school agricultural shops, community college small-engine repair classes, and community tool clinics are often the best recipients for riders that do not run, or for older machines retail stores cannot resell.

Intake criteria: Programs value riders as teaching equipment, even non-running ones, provided they are complete enough to work on. Contact the program instructor directly, explain the machine's condition honestly, and ask whether they can collect it.

COMMUNITY & DIRECT GIVING

5. Community Grounds Programs and Give-Away Platforms

A working rider can serve a community garden network, a neighborhood association, or a church grounds crew that maintains large areas. Freecycle and Buy Nothing groups are another direct route: the recipient usually collects the machine themselves, which solves your transport problem.

Intake criteria: Grounds programs need to confirm they have storage, a trained operator, and a use for a rider specifically — a small garden plot does not. For give-away platforms, describe the rider's condition, fuel type, and whether it runs, and be clear about pickup requirements.

Second, paid junk-removal services will also take a rider, but that is removal, not donation — you pay, and the machine may be scrapped rather than reused. If reuse matters to you, try the charitable routes first.

How to Prepare a Riding Mower for Donation

  • Clean it: Remove grass buildup from the deck, clear debris from the engine area, and wipe down the seat and controls.
  • Drain fuel and oil: For gas riders, drain the tank and engine oil following the manufacturer's manual and local disposal rules. Never pour fluids onto soil or into drains.
  • Check the battery: Electric-start riders need a working battery; include the charger for battery-powered models. A dead battery is a common reason a rider is refused.
  • Check tires and deck: Tires should hold air, and the cutting deck should be intact, not rusted through. Blades should be present and secure.
  • Gather keys and manuals: Include the ignition key, any safety key, and the operator manual if you have it. A rider without a key is much harder to place.
  • Test it honestly: Start the rider and run it briefly. Note any problems — smoke, stalling, weak drive — and disclose them. Organizations would rather know upfront than discover a fault at the curb.
  • Plan the move: Confirm the receiving organization's space, loading access, and whether you need ramps or a trailer. Riders typically weigh several hundred pounds.

What About a Riding Mower That Does Not Run?

A non-running rider still has options, but retail thrift stores are usually not one of them, because they cannot repair or resell it. Your best routes, in order:

  • Vocational programs: Small-engine repair classes actively seek non-running riders as training machines. Call the program directly.
  • Repair clinics and tool libraries: Some community repair events accept riders for refurbishment and community use.
  • Parts and give-away listings: A rider with a good deck, tires, or engine parts may interest a hobbyist or small repair shop. Describe faults honestly.
  • Scrap metal recycling: If the rider is unsafe, seized, or stripped, scrap recycling is the responsible end. Drain all fluids, remove the battery for separate handling, and follow local hazardous-waste rules.

If you are unsure whether your rider counts as repairable, our guide on donating broken garden tools explains the condition thresholds charities apply.

Tax Receipts: What to Know

Donations to qualified nonprofits such as Habitat ReStores and Salvation Army centers may be tax-deductible if you itemize, subject to IRS rules and limits. The receiving organization cannot value your rider for you; documenting fair market value is your responsibility. Use our fair market value estimator for a planning range, keep your signed receipt, and consult a qualified tax professional. Frequently Asked Questions

Where can I donate a riding lawn mower?

Start with organizations that have trucks: Habitat for Humanity ReStores and Salvation Army centers (via satruck.org in select ZIP codes) are the most realistic options for a working rider. Goodwill varies by location and is often drop-off only. Vocational programs are the best route for non-running riders. Always confirm acceptance and pickup availability with the specific organization first.

Does Habitat for Humanity pick up riding mowers?

Some ReStores offer pickup for large items, including riding mowers, but availability varies by store. Call your local ReStore, describe the rider's size and condition, and ask whether their truck can collect it. Push mowers are generally drop-off only; riders are the exception because of their size.

Does Salvation Army pick up riding mowers?

In select ZIP codes, yes, through satruck.org. Riding mowers are among the items most likely to qualify for a truck visit because donors usually cannot transport them. The rider should be working and have fuel drained if it is a gas model. If online scheduling shows no availability, call your local center.

Does Goodwill take riding lawn mowers?

It varies widely. Some larger Goodwill locations accept working riders, but many stores are drop-off only and lack the space to receive them. Gas riders must have fuel and oil fully drained. Verify with your specific store before arranging transport.

Can I donate a riding mower that does not run?

Retail thrift stores usually cannot, because they do not repair equipment. Vocational schools, community college small-engine programs, and repair clinics often welcome non-running riders as training projects. If the rider is unsafe or beyond repair, drain all fluids and take it to scrap metal recycling.

Can I get a tax deduction for donating a riding mower?

Donations to qualified nonprofits may be tax-deductible if you itemize deductions, subject to IRS limits. You must document fair market value yourself and keep the signed receipt. Consult a qualified tax professional for your situation.

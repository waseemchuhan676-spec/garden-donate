What Garden Tools Can You Donate?

Clean, safe and usable items are generally the best candidates, but every organization makes its own decision.

Shovels, Rakes & Hoes

Common hand tools such as shovels, rakes, hoes, spades and trowels may be useful to community programs and reuse organizations.

Pruning Tools & Shears

Pruning shears, garden shears, cultivators and similar tools can be useful when they are safe and in usable condition.

Wheelbarrows & Supplies

Wheelbarrows, hoses, pots, planters, stakes and other clean reusable gardening supplies may have local reuse opportunities.

Lawn Equipment

Lawn mowers, string trimmers, hedge trimmers, leaf blowers and edgers can have stricter acceptance requirements.

Power Tools

Powered equipment should be inspected carefully and checked with the receiving organization before transport.

Broken or Rusty Tools

Light wear may be acceptable, but severely damaged or unsafe tools may be better suited to repair, recycling or another disposal route.

Before You Donate

  • Clean off soil, grass and loose debris.
  • Check handles, blades, cords, switches and moving parts.
  • Contact the organization first.
  • Confirm it accepts your exact item.
  • Check opening hours and pickup/drop-off rules.
  • Keep accessories together when possible.
Important: The receiving organization decides what it can accept.

Garden Tool Donation Guide

Practical answers for people looking to give garden tools, lawn equipment and gardening supplies a useful second life.

Where can I donate garden tools near me?

Start with the local search categories above: community gardens, charities, reuse centers, tool libraries, thrift organizations, Habitat ReStore locations and other nonprofits. Use your ZIP code or city to create a focused search, then confirm the exact location before visiting.

Who accepts old garden tools?

Old tools that are safe and usable may be suitable for community gardens, reuse programs, charities, tool libraries and some thrift organizations. Age alone does not determine acceptance; condition and local policy matter.

Can I donate used shovels and rakes?

Often, clean and usable hand tools are among the easier garden items to reuse. Inspect handles and working parts, remove dirt and ask the receiving organization whether it accepts the specific item.

Does Habitat ReStore accept garden tools?

Some ReStore locations may accept usable tools or home-improvement items. Policies differ by location, so use the official donation information and confirm with the local ReStore before making a trip.

Where can I donate lawn equipment?

Possible routes include charities, reuse organizations, community programs and other local nonprofits. Powered equipment can require special handling, so confirm fuel, battery, condition, pickup and transport requirements first.

What should I do with old gardening tools?

If the tool is safe and usable, donation or direct community reuse can be a good option. If it is unsafe or severely damaged, consider repair, an appropriate recycling program or another local disposal route.

Frequently Asked Questions

Where can I donate garden tools near me?

Start with local charities, community gardens, Habitat ReStore locations, thrift or reuse organizations, tool libraries, schools and nonprofits. Enter your ZIP code or city above to create local search options, then confirm the exact organization's current donation policy before visiting.

Who accepts old garden tools?

Possible options include reuse centers, community gardens, tool libraries, local charities, thrift organizations and some Habitat ReStore or Goodwill locations. Acceptance varies by local branch and item.

Can I donate used shovels and rakes?

Clean, safe and usable shovels, rakes and similar hand tools may be accepted by community programs, reuse organizations, charities or tool libraries. Confirm the receiving organization's policy first.

Does Habitat ReStore accept garden tools?

Some Habitat ReStore locations may accept usable tools or home-improvement items, but donation rules differ by location. Check the official local ReStore guidance before donating.

Where can I donate lawn equipment?

Some charities, reuse programs and community organizations may accept lawn equipment. Powered equipment can have additional requirements, so confirm acceptance, condition, fuel requirements and pickup or drop-off rules first.

What should I do with old gardening tools?

If an old tool is safe and usable, consider donating or reusing it. Clean and inspect it first. Broken or unsafe items may need repair, recycling or another appropriate disposal route.

Should I clean garden tools before donating?

Yes. Remove soil, grass and loose debris, dry the item, inspect it for damage and keep important accessories together when possible.

Can donated garden tools qualify for a tax deduction?

A donation may qualify under applicable tax rules when requirements are met. Keep appropriate documentation and consult current official tax guidance or a qualified tax professional. This website does not determine deductibility.

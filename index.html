What should usually NOT be donated?

  • Tools with exposed wiring or serious safety defects
  • Equipment leaking fuel, oil or other hazardous fluids
  • Severely broken or badly rusted tools
  • Items recalled for a safety defect
  • Used pesticide/herbicide containers or hazardous chemicals
Important: Acceptance rules are set by each organization and can differ between locations.

Donation Prep Checklist

Prepare your tools before drop-off or pickup.

1. Clean

Remove soil, grass and debris. Wipe metal surfaces and dry everything thoroughly.

2. Inspect

Check handles, blades, cords, switches and moving parts. Separate anything unsafe.

3. Group

Bundle similar hand tools and keep accessories, chargers and attachments together.

4. Check Policy

Call the exact donation center before arriving. Local acceptance rules can be different.

5. Photograph

For large-item pickup, keep clear photos and an item list ready if the organization requests them.

6. Ask for Receipt

If you need donation documentation, request the appropriate receipt or acknowledgment from the organization.

Frequently Asked Questions

Can I donate broken garden tools?

Usually, charities prefer safe and usable items. Severely broken or unsafe tools are often better handled through repair, reuse or recycling programs.

Can I donate a lawn mower?

Some organizations accept working lawn equipment, but policies vary. Confirm whether the specific location accepts mowers and whether pickup is available.

Do all Habitat ReStores accept the same items?

No. Habitat states that each ReStore is unique and recommends contacting the local store to confirm what it accepts and its donation hours.

Can I get a tax deduction for donated tools?

Potentially, if the donation is made to a qualifying organization and you meet applicable tax requirements. Keep the organization's acknowledgment and consult current IRS guidance or a tax professional for your situation.

Where should I donate garden tools if there is no charity nearby?

Try community gardens, schools, neighborhood gardening groups, tool libraries, local reuse programs or appropriate recycling facilities. Contact them first to confirm they want the items.

What Should Usually Not Be Donated?

  • Tools with exposed wiring or serious safety defects
  • Equipment leaking fuel, oil or other hazardous fluids
  • Severely broken or badly rusted tools
  • Items recalled for a safety defect
  • Used pesticide or herbicide containers and hazardous chemicals
Important: Acceptance rules are set by each organization and can differ between locations.

Donation Prep Checklist

Prepare your tools before drop-off or pickup.

1. Clean

Remove soil, grass and debris. Wipe metal surfaces and dry everything thoroughly.

2. Inspect

Check handles, blades, cords, switches and moving parts. Separate anything unsafe.

3. Group

Bundle similar hand tools and keep accessories, chargers and attachments together.

4. Check Policy

Call the exact donation center before arriving. Local acceptance rules can be different.

5. Photograph

For large-item pickup, keep clear photos and an item list ready if the organization requests them.

6. Ask for a Receipt

If you need donation documentation, request the appropriate receipt or acknowledgment from the organization.

How to Donate Garden Tools and Lawn Equipment

A practical guide to finding a useful second home for gardening equipment.

If you are wondering where to donate garden tools, start by deciding whether the item is safe, usable and likely to be useful to another person or organization. Hand tools, working lawn equipment, watering supplies and other gardening items can sometimes be reused instead of being discarded. The best destination depends on the item, its condition and the acceptance rules in your area.

Start With the Condition of the Item

Before searching for a donation center, inspect the tool carefully. A usable shovel, rake, pruning tool or wheelbarrow may only need cleaning. Power equipment deserves additional checks because damaged cords, leaking fuel or mechanical safety problems can make an item unsuitable for donation. If an item is unsafe, repair or recycling may be more appropriate than passing the problem to a charity.

Consider Community-Based Donation Options

Charities are not the only places that may need gardening supplies. Community gardens, school gardens, youth programs and neighborhood reuse projects can sometimes benefit from usable hand tools, pots, hoses and other supplies. These options can be especially helpful when you want donated equipment to stay in active use within your community.

Prepare Lawn Equipment Carefully

Lawn mowers, trimmers and blowers can have stricter acceptance requirements. Confirm whether the organization accepts the equipment and whether it must be in working order. Do not assume that every donation center can handle fuel-powered or large equipment. Follow the receiving organization's instructions and local safety rules before transport.

Why Reuse Garden Tools?

Extending the useful life of durable equipment can reduce waste and help useful items reach people or projects that need them. Donation is most valuable when the recipient can safely use the item. Taking a few minutes to clean, inspect and organize tools can make the donation process easier for both you and the receiving organization.

Frequently Asked Questions

Can I donate broken garden tools?

Usually, charities prefer safe and usable items. Severely broken or unsafe tools are often better handled through repair, reuse or recycling programs.

Can I donate a lawn mower?

Some organizations accept working lawn equipment, but policies vary. Confirm whether the specific location accepts mowers and whether pickup is available.

Do all Habitat ReStores accept the same items?

No. Local ReStores can have different acceptance rules, so contact the specific store to confirm what it accepts and its donation hours.

Can I get a tax deduction for donated tools?

Potentially, depending on the receiving organization and applicable requirements. Keep appropriate documentation and consult current official guidance or a tax professional for your situation.

Where should I donate garden tools if there is no charity nearby?

Try community gardens, schools, neighborhood gardening groups, tool libraries, local reuse programs or appropriate recycling facilities. Contact them first to confirm they want the items.

Should I clean garden tools before donating them?

Yes. Removing soil, grass and loose debris makes items easier to inspect and handle. Clean, dry and reasonably presentable equipment is generally easier for an organization to process.
